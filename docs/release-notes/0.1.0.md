Solara is a Mac browser with Arc's sidebar and Dia's polish, on Chrome's own engine (Chromium 156) built from source.
It has no account, no AI features and no telemetry; uBlock Origin is built in, extensions come from the Chrome Web
Store, and sync, if you want it, runs through your own iCloud Drive. It needs macOS 26 or later.

**Install this version by hand.** 0.1.0 is the first release signed and notarized under Solara's own developer
account, with a new app identity, so 0.0.1 doesn't update to it: download the DMG and replace the app. It starts with
a fresh profile and doesn't bring over 0.0.1's tabs or data. From 0.1.0 on, updates install themselves.

## New

- **Solara is signed with its own Developer ID and notarized by Apple.** macOS opens it without a trip to Privacy &
  Security, and camera, microphone and other permissions stay granted across updates.
- **Saved passwords, cookies and cards are locked with a key in your login keychain**, not Chrome's built-in one.
- **Solara's sun is on every icon.** The old hills artwork is gone from the app and from the icons Chrome draws, such
  as the one on notification prompts.

## Fixed

- **Deleting a profile removes all of its data.** Its pages' data, its history and its sync state go together, and a
  deletion that couldn't finish while the profile was open finishes on the next launch.
- **Nothing from a closed private window comes back.** Undo and restore no longer keep a private session's pages,
  find text or downloads after its last window closes.
- **Bookmark sync no longer stalls on an unusual bookmark from another Mac**, and bookmarks keep the same order on
  every Mac.
- **Full screen reveals just the menu bar.** Moving the pointer to the top no longer slides a grey band in under it.
- **Reopening or opening a tab while a window is closing no longer crashes the browser.**

## Smaller

- The line under Pinned shows only when there are pinned tabs above the list.
- Switching tabs does less work: page controls with nothing to show are no longer set up on every switch.

## Not yet

- **No passkeys.** Signing in with a passkey stored in iCloud Keychain needs a permission Apple hasn't granted yet;
  a later release turns it on.
